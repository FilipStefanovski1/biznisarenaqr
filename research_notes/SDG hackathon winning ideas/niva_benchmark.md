# Niva benchmark: abandoned family farmland, satellite detection and remote leasing (North Macedonia, then the Western Balkans and Southeast EU)

Research date: 2026-10-09. Rules used here: every claim in "Cited Findings" has a URL. "Inferences" are my own reasoning. Data older than about 2023 is flagged **[OLD]**. Some sources were read only as search-result snippets, not full text. Those are flagged **[snippet]**.

---

## 1. Abandonment evidence: is "about 32% of arable land abandoned" (FAO 2023) correct? What causes it? How fragmented is the land?

### Takeaway
Yes, broadly. FAO's 2023 analysis gives roughly 32% of arable land abandoned, and the figure was still being quoted at a Ministry of Agriculture, Economic Chamber and FAO event in March 2026. Two caveats:
- The figure is a national average across **both state and private** land.
- About 30–40% of farmland is state-owned, so not all abandoned land belongs to diaspora heirs.

Fragmentation is severe: farms average 1.8–1.9 ha spread over 5–7 parcels, and parcels in consolidation areas averaged 0.38–1.23 ha before consolidation.

### Cited Findings
- FAO says approximately 32% of arable land in North Macedonia has been abandoned, far above the level in most other European countries. Abandonment is higher in the west of the country and in mountainous municipalities, and lower where soil quality is high. — [FAO REU news (via search) [snippet]](https://sfcs.fao.org/europe/news/detail/fao-continues-push-to-address-land-abandonment-in-north-macedonia/en); underlying report: [FAO cc4778en PDF](https://openknowledge.fao.org/3/cc4778en/cc4778en.pdf). The PDF returned HTTP 403, so the methodology could not be verified directly.
- How FAO defines abandoned land: agricultural land previously used as arable land, vineyards or orchards that is no longer used for production. The report notes that **North Macedonian law has no legal definition of land abandonment.** — [FAO cc4778en [snippet]](https://www.fao.org/3/cc4778en/cc4778en.pdf)
- Farm structure: average farm size is 1.8–1.9 ha, with on average 5–7 parcels per farm. — [FAO cc4778en [snippet]](https://www.fao.org/3/cc4778en/cc4778en.pdf)
- About 40% of arable agricultural land is state-owned. — [FAO cc4778en [snippet]](https://www.fao.org/3/cc4778en/cc4778en.pdf). An older source gives about 30%: state-owned land is 237,882 ha, or about 30% of agricultural land, and about 140,000 ha of it is leased to more than 6,000 tenants. — [FAO LANDNET 2013 presentation [OLD]](https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/user_upload/reu/europe/documents/LANDNET/2013_2/2.2_Macedonia_en.pdf). The two shares conflict, probably because of different denominators (arable land vs all agricultural land) and different years.
- FAO names rural outmigration as a driver of widespread abandonment of arable land. — [FAO cc4778en [snippet]](https://www.fao.org/3/cc4778en/cc4778en.pdf)
- At a March 2026 event run by the Economic Chamber with the Ministry of Agriculture (MAFWE) and FAO, speakers again cited the 32% figure and called for stronger implementation of the land consolidation strategy. — [Economic Chamber of North Macedonia](https://mchamber.mk/en/news/index/1023)
- Fragmentation examples from land consolidation projects:
  - Egri: 874 parcels were reshaped into 260, and average parcel size rose from 0.38 ha to 1.30 ha. The area covered 336 ha and 214 farmers. — [FAO, Feb 2020](https://www.fao.org/europe/news/detail/The-first-majority-based-land-consolidation-plan-adopted-in-Egri-North-Macedonia-/en)
  - Dabjani: 602 parcels became 127, and average parcel size rose from 1.23 ha to 5.84 ha. — [FAO MAINLAND page](https://www.fao.org/in-action/mainstreaming-national-land-consolidation-programme/en/)
- Average holdings are under 2 ha, which "limits rural growth" and hurts productivity and competitiveness. — [FAO MAINLAND](https://www.fao.org/in-action/mainstreaming-national-land-consolidation-programme/en/)
- Co-ownership between the state and private owners has blocked about 2,000 private landowners in Pelagonija from using their land for decades. An FAO project is targeting this problem. — [FAO REU](https://sfcs.fao.org/europe/news/detail/fao-project-targets-resolving-public-private-co-ownership-to-agricultural-land-and-improved-agricultural-land-management/en)

### Inferences
- The 32% figure is solid enough for a benchmark headline. However, the share of abandoned land that is **private, owned by emigrants or heirs, and inside reasonable distance of active farmers** is unknown, and it is probably much smaller than 32%:
  - About a third of farmland is state-owned. The state already leases that land through tenders, which is a separate channel Niva cannot easily capture.
  - Abandonment is concentrated in mountainous and low-quality-soil areas, where demand from young farmers is weakest.
- "Unresolved inheritance" is a plausible root cause and is widely assumed. However, I found **no quantitative source** for North Macedonia, such as the share of cadastral parcels still registered to deceased owners.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full FAO cc4778en methodology (it returned 403). It is unknown whether the 32% comes from a survey, municipal data or remote sensing, and what base year it uses.
- No statistic was found on the share of agricultural parcels with owners abroad or with unresolved probate.
- No updated national abandonment estimate after 2023 was found.

## 2. Legal: majority-consent leasing for co-owned parcels, inheritance, leases and subsidies, and the 3x property tax

### Takeaway
**The core legal premise of the idea, that a co-owned parcel can be leased with majority consent, appears to be FALSE under North Macedonia's general property law.**
- Article 37 of the Law on Ownership and Other Real Property Rights lists leasing as an extraordinary act that needs the consent of **all** co-owners.
- If the co-owners cannot agree, the only fallback is a court decision in non-contentious proceedings, or dissolving the co-ownership.
- Majority-based decisions do exist, but only inside formal **land consolidation** procedures under the separate consolidation law.

The 3x tax on unused agricultural land was adopted in 2021 and took effect on 1 January 2022. I found no evidence of how, or whether, municipalities identify unused land in practice.

### Cited Findings
- **Law on Ownership and Other Real Property Rights** (Official Gazette 18/2001, as published; later amendments not checked):
  - **Article 36:** ordinary management requires consent of co-owners whose shares together exceed half the value of the property. Original wording: «повеќе од половината на вредноста на стварта».
  - **Article 37:** acts beyond ordinary management require the consent of **all co-owners**. The listed acts include «отуѓување на целата ствар, промена на намената на стварта, **издавање на стварта во закуп**, засновање залог…», meaning alienation of the whole property, change of use, **leasing**, and creating a pledge. If consent is not reached, Article 36(2) applies: the **court decides**. The court may also dissolve the co-ownership.
  - **Article 38:** co-owners may appoint a manager, who can be a third party.
  - **Article 45:** that manager carries out majority decisions for ordinary management, but needs a unanimous decision for extraordinary management.
  - Source: [Law text (pravo.org.mk copy hosted by Gazi Baba municipality)](https://business.gazibaba.gov.mk/media/pdf/zakonska_regulativa/pravni_raboti/sopstvenost_i_drugi_stvarni_prava/1-sopstvenost-i-drugi-stvarni-prava.pdf). This is the 2001 base text. Amendments were not reviewed.
- Majority-based decisions do exist under the land consolidation regime. In Egri in 2020, "the first majority-based land consolidation plan" was adopted by a qualified majority of landowners. — [FAO](https://www.fao.org/europe/news/detail/The-first-majority-based-land-consolidation-plan-adopted-in-Egri-North-Macedonia-/en). The exact statutory threshold was not given. Law reference: [Law on Consolidation of Agricultural Land (FAOLEX/LEAP)](https://leap.unep.org/en/countries/mk/national-legislation/law-consolidation-agricultural-land)
- Ownership of real estate acquired by legal transaction arises on registration in the cadastre, which the Agency for Real Estate Cadastre maintains. — [UNITE eprints](https://eprints.unite.edu.mk/694/)
- Foreign persons may acquire long-term leases of agricultural land, subject to reciprocity and the consent of the Minister of Justice. — [CEE Legal Matters 2021 [OLD]](https://ceelm.com/real-estate-2021/real-estate-north-macedonia-2021)
- **Subsidies and leases:**
  - The direct-payments criteria regulation lists a usufruct or lease contract on state or private agricultural land among the documents an applicant submits. — [FAOLEX mac218115 [snippet, year unclear]](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/mac218115.pdf)
  - A 2021 amendment caps the area a farmer can claim at the cadastral parcel areas recorded in the **Single Register of Agricultural Holdings** on the application date, and payments are checked against the Land Parcel Identification System. — [AFSARD/IPARDPA 2021 regulation amendment](https://www.ipardpa.gov.mk/Upload/Documents/Уредба%20за%20измен%20и%20допол%20на%20уредбата%20за%20поблиските%20критериуми%2024032021.pdf)
  - Only agricultural holdings registered in the Single Register are eligible for 2024 crop payments. — [RCC WBIIP](https://www.wbiip.rcc.int/incentives/275/direct-financial-support-in-agriculture-crop-production)
- **3x property tax:**
  - The Assembly adopted amendments to the Law on Property Taxes that triple the tax rate for real estate neither used nor leased for more than six months a year, and for **agricultural land not used for agricultural production**. They took effect on 1 January 2022. Base rates are 0.10–0.20%.
  - A fivefold increase applies to owners who fail to declare that they do not use a property.
  - Source: [Karanović & Partners, July 2021](https://karanovicpartners.com/news/north-macedonia-latest-amendments-to-the-property-taxes-legislation); published in the Official Gazette on 6 July 2021 according to [Orbitax](https://www.orbitax.com/news/archive.php/North-Macedonia-Increasing-Pro-47036)
  - The Karanović note does **not** say how unused status is determined (declaration, inspection, or something else). Its wording also swings between "adopted" and "proposed".
- Municipalities set property tax rates by council decision under Articles 6, 16 and 22 of the Law on Property Taxes. Example: Kumanovo's 2026 rate decision. — [Kumanovo decision, 2026](https://kumanovo.gov.mk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/ОДЛУКА-за-утврдување-на-висината-на-стапките-на-данок-на-имот-данок-на-наследство-и-подарок-и-данок-на-промет-на-недвижности.pdf)

### Inferences
- **This is the single biggest risk to the business model.** Diaspora-heir parcels are exactly the ones most likely to have several co-owners or unresolved heirs. Under Article 37, one silent or unreachable heir can block a lawful lease. Niva would need one of these:
  - every heir to sign, which is costly and needs remote notarisation or powers of attorney from abroad;
  - a court order in non-contentious proceedings;
  - the parcel to be routed through a formal land consolidation procedure, which is state-led and slow;
  - a change in the law.

  Informal handshake leases are common in the region, but they cannot support subsidy claims and they leave the young farmer exposed.
- Subsidy eligibility appears to depend on a documented right of use (a lease or usufruct contract) plus entry in the Single Register and LPIS. If so, a formal, documented lease is the main value Niva adds for the farmer. That also makes the unanimity rule above binding rather than a technicality.
- The 3x tax gives owners a reason to show their land is "used". A cheap lease through Niva could be marketed as tax avoidance. But the tax amount is probably small, because base rates of 0.1–0.2% are applied to municipal valuations of agricultural land. The pull may therefore be weak unless municipalities enforce it actively.
- Unverified recollection: the Law on Property Taxes may exempt agricultural land *used* for agricultural production. If so, unused land is treated as a deviation from that exemption. This needs checking against the statute.

### Gaps
- No post-2001 consolidated text of the Law on Ownership was checked. Amendments, or a special rule in the Law on Agricultural Land (consolidated through Official Gazette 91/2023, with 2024 amendments), could alter Article 37 for agricultural land. A local lawyer should confirm.
- No statistic was found on how many inheritance proceedings are unresolved or how many parcels are still registered to deceased owners in North Macedonia. The only detailed source on this pattern is about Kosovo.
- Whether the 2026 direct-payments regulation requires the lease to be **registered** (with a notary or the cadastre) or only **submitted** was not confirmed.
- No evidence was found on municipal **enforcement** of the 3x tax: how many municipalities apply it, how they detect unused land, or how much revenue it brings in. No source says satellite data is used. The tax's current status after 2022 was not confirmed.

## 3. Market: lease prices, diaspora size, land consolidation projects, and any state land bank or e-platform

### Takeaway
- **Lease prices are low.** Starting bids for state land tenders are €60–120/ha/year, compared with an EU average rent of €173/ha in 2023. No reliable figure for private-land rent was found.
- **The diaspora is large:** about 600,000–700,000 people, or roughly 25% of the population.
- FAO and the EU have funded land consolidation since 2017: MAINLAND, then "Enhancing Land Consolidation" from August 2022 to January 2026.
- No public e-platform or land bank for leasing **private** land was found.

### Cited Findings
- Minimum tender prices for leasing **state** land by soil class, per ha per year:
  - classes 1–2: €120
  - classes 3–4: €90
  - classes 5–6: €80
  - classes 7–8: €60

  — [HortiDaily [date not shown, likely OLD]](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/6019876/released-call-for-the-lease-of-land-over-3-ha-in-macedonia/)
- The EU average rent paid to owners was €173 per ha per year in 2023. — [BTA, citing Eurostat](https://www.bta.bg/en/news/822808-eurostat-data-arable-land-rents-in-2023)
- The farmland market is "underperforming and lacks transparency", with limited price information. Average sale price was €1/m² from desk research and €1.53/m² from a field survey, i.e. about €10,000–15,300/ha. — [UKIM repository [OLD, about 2013 data]](https://repository.ukim.mk/handle/20.500.12188/7624?mode=full)
- State land leases run 10–50 years, and state land cannot be sold under the 2007 Agricultural Land Law. — [FAO LANDNET 2013 [OLD]](https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/user_upload/reu/europe/documents/LANDNET/2013_2/2.2_Macedonia_en.pdf)
- **Diaspora size estimates:**
  - about 600,000 citizens abroad (AFP, Jan 2022, citing World Bank and government data) — [Ahram/AFP](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/454475.aspx)
  - 693,896 emigrants, about 25% of the population (UN DESA 2020) — [ECONDIAS factsheet 2024](https://diasporafordevelopment.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/NM-Factsheet-v.2.pdf)
  - 0.69 million, a 25% emigration rate (2020), and 18% of the population living in OECD countries — [OECD](https://search.oecd.org/south-east-europe/programme/Economies-2-Pagers_MKD_INTERACTIVE.pdf)
- **MAINLAND (2017–2022):** €2.5 million from the EU and €360,000 from FAO. It built capacity at MAFWE and reduced fragmentation in its pilot areas. — [FAO MAINLAND](https://www.fao.org/in-action/mainstreaming-national-land-consolidation-programme/en/)
- **"Enhancing Land Consolidation in North Macedonia" (EU/FAO, Aug 2022 – Jan 2026):**
  - Work covered the Dabjani (Dolneni) and Chiflik (Cheshinovo-Obleshevo) projects, the policy and legal framework, and infrastructure.
  - "Reduced area of abandoned land" is a stated impact.
  - It also refers to "developing agricultural land markets" and "updating land ownership data".
  - Sources: [FAO project background](https://www.fao.org/in-action/enhancing-land-consolidation/background/en); [UN North Macedonia](https://northmacedonia.un.org/en/node/206880); [EEAS](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/north-macedonia/second-phase-eu-supported-land-consolidation-process-greater-parcels-bring-greater-yields-%E2%80%93-and_en)

### Inferences
- **Unit economics are thin.** Assume a private-land rent of about €80–200/ha/year and a typical heir parcel of 0.4–1.5 ha. A 10–15% service fee then yields about €5–45 per parcel per year. That cannot cover the cost of tracing heirs, gathering signatures and notarisation, which likely runs to tens or hundreds of euros per parcel. Revenue must come from:
  - aggregating many parcels per farmer;
  - the diaspora subscription; or
  - B2G (municipal and ministry) contracts.
- **The diaspora subscription is the most plausible revenue line,** because the diaspora is large (about 25% of the population) and attached to its land. But willingness to pay for land that earns about €100/ha is doubtful. The pitch would have to be about tax avoidance, legal title clean-up and peace of mind, not rental income.
- **FAO/EU consolidation projects are potential partners or data sources rather than competitors.** They also show the government already sees abandonment and fragmentation as a policy problem, which helps a B2G sale.

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) market rent for **private** land in North Macedonia was found.
- No evidence was found of a MAFWE e-platform or land bank for **private** land leasing. State land is leased through public tenders, but no online platform for private land was identified.
- The status of the amendments to the Law on Agricultural Land that FAO expected in 2023 is unknown.

## 4. Competitors: Balkan platforms, European land banks, and satellite-based abandonment startups

### Takeaway
I found **no Balkan or Southeast European platform** that matches private farmland owners with farmers for leasing. The closest analogues are public or non-profit land banks and mobility services in Western Europe. They have taken years to reach modest scale:
- Galicia: about 6,000–7,000 ha after more than 15 years.
- Ireland: 15,000 ha in its first 4 years.

The only satellite-based abandoned-farmland company found is Japan's Sagri. Its abandonment product is sold to local governments.

### Cited Findings
- **Banco de Terras de Galicia (Spain, since 2007):**
  - Aim: to stop farmland abandonment and mobilise land for farming, forestry and other uses. — [Urban Agenda for the EU case study (2026)](https://www.urbanagenda.urban-initiative.eu/sites/default/files/2026-06/Banco%20de%20Terras%20de%20Galicia%2C%20Spain.pdf)
  - Xunta (Galician regional government) notice: about 19,000 plots and more than 7,000 ha offered for rent, with 6,856 plots rented or in process. The notice is undated. — [Xunta](https://www.xunta.gal/gl/notas-de-prensa/-/nova/021394/medio-rural-publica-unha-nova-orde-que-facilita-tramites-arrendamento-preto-dun)
  - An earlier report gives 12,355 properties and 5,785 ha, with about a quarter rented. Registered properties were 6,722 in 2008. AGADER, the Galician rural development agency, manages 91.3% of the properties. — [AgroNews Castilla y León [likely about 2022]](https://www.agronewscastillayleon.com/cerca-de-6000-hectareas-estan-inscritas-en-el-banco-de-tierras-de-galicia)
- **Bolsa Nacional de Terras (Portugal):** about 100,000 ha of farmland are unused. One example allocation gave 19 state properties to 15 new tenants, 67% of them young farmers, with two years rent-free. — [Águeda municipality presentation [undated, OLD]](https://www.cm-agueda.pt/cmagueda/uploads/writer_file/document/3040/Apresentac_a_o_Bolsa_de_Terras_Nuno_Russo.pdf)
- **Land Mobility Service (Ireland):**
  - Set up in 2014 by Macra and the FBD Trust. — [AgriLand](https://www.agriland.ie/farming-news/macra-launches-land-mobility-service/)
  - More than 400 arrangements covering 15,000 ha in 2014–2017. — [ENRD presentation 2018 [OLD]](https://ec.europa.eu/enrd/sites/default/files/w32_attracting_young_farmers_ireland_land_mobility_duggan_finn.pdf)
  - In 2025, its manager said landowners were holding off on stepping back from farming. — [Farmers Journal](https://www.farmersjournal.ie/news/news/collaborative-farming-options-are-on-the-increase-881308)
- **Perspektive Landwirtschaft (Austria):** an online platform plus events that match farms without successors with newcomers. It is funded partly by the federal agriculture ministry through Rural Development funds. — [EU CAP Network, Dec 2024](https://eu-cap-network.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-12/eu-cap-network-tg-gr-perspektive-landwirtschaft-initiative.pdf)
- **Sagri (Japan):**
  - Its "Actaba" product uses machine learning and satellite data to visualise abandoned farmland, and the company claims more than 90% accuracy. — [Tokyo Updates](https://www.tokyoupdates.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/en/post-1134/); [The Bridge, seed round 2021](https://thebridge.jp/en/2021/06/sagri-seed-round-funding)
- **Farm Legacy (Finland, 2025):** an AI-assisted farm succession matching platform that does not use satellite data. — [The Hub](https://thehub.io/startups/farm-legacy)

### Inferences
- **No direct competitor appears to exist in North Macedonia or the Western Balkans.** That is good for novelty but also a warning sign: the Western European analogues are mostly **publicly funded** (Galicia, Portugal, Austria, Ireland's ministry and co-op support). That suggests a stand-alone private business model is hard, and the realistic route is a public-private or B2G arrangement.
- **Sagri is the strongest proof that "satellite-detected abandonment sold to municipalities" is a real product.** Japan has a legal and administrative push (municipal farmland committees must survey idle land every year), and North Macedonia lacks an equivalent. In North Macedonia the 3x tax is the only hook for that kind of demand.
- The Galicia figures (19,000 plots for about 7,000 ha, so about 0.37 ha per plot) show how micro-fragmented land-bank inventory tends to be. That is directly relevant to whether Niva can aggregate parcels into farmable blocks.

### Gaps
- Catalonia's land bank (Banc de Terres) was not researched.
- No search was done for Serbian, Croatian, Bulgarian, Albanian or Kosovar private-land lease platforms (for example classifieds sites or agri-marketplaces). Their absence is unconfirmed.
- No current Portuguese Bolsa de Terras statistics were found.
- No other European satellite-abandonment startups were found. The query was limited and is not exhaustive.

## 5. Technical feasibility: can Sentinel-2 NDVI time series reliably detect abandoned cropland?

### Takeaway
**Yes, at research grade.**
- Published Sentinel-2 and Landsat abandonment maps report overall accuracies of roughly 80–98%.
- For the abandoned class itself, accuracy is usually lower, about 78–87%.
- Separating abandoned arable land from extensively used grassland and fallow is the weak point.

Very small parcels (under 0.4 ha) and fallow rotations mean field-level verification will still be needed. The detection layer is feasible. It is not the bottleneck.

### Cited Findings
- **Carpathians (Landsat, SVM):** overall accuracy 90.9%, kappa 0.82. 16.1% of farmland used in the socialist era had been abandoned. — Kuemmerle et al. 2008, Ecosystems [OLD] — [HU Berlin EOLab](https://eolab.geographie.hu-berlin.de/publication/kuemmerle_2008c/)
- **Eastern Europe (Landsat, SVM):** conditional kappa of 90% for "abandoned arable land" but only 72% for "abandoned managed grassland" when six image dates were used. Accuracy fell sharply with fewer image dates. — [Univ. of Copenhagen record](https://test01.ku.dk/:obvius/pureproxy/3974268/en/publications/the-effect-of-landsat-etmetm-image-acquisition-dates-on-the-detec)
- **Central and Eastern Europe (MODIS):** abandonment was mapped at regional scale from MODIS NDVI for 2004–2006. — Alcantara et al. 2013, ERL [OLD] — [IOP](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/8/3/035035/meta). A review reports this study reached about 65% accuracy, versus 87–97% for parcel-level studies. — [MDPI Land 2019](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/land/land-08-00129/article_deploy/land-08-00129.pdf)
- **Caucasus (Landsat, spatio-temporal segmentation):** 97 ± 1% overall accuracy at object level, versus 82 ± 3% for pixel-level change detection. — Yin et al. 2018, RSE — [HU Berlin](https://biogeo.pages.cms.hu-berlin.de/website/publication/2018-01-01_mapping_agricultural/)
- **Sentinel-2, object-level, China:** 93 ± 5% overall, with 81 ± 2% for the abandoned-cropland class. — [IGSNRR CAS 2025](https://igsnrr.cas.cn/sourcedb/zw/lw/202508/t20250826_7913701.html)
- **Harmonized Landsat–Sentinel-2, mountainous terrain:** 78.2% producer's accuracy and 81.3% user's accuracy for abandonment. — [DOAJ, Int. J. Applied Earth Obs. Geoinf. 2024](https://doaj.org/article/2325444d641c451a9a62bcd4909f9546)
- **Landsat 8 + Sentinel-2 + Sentinel-1 random forest (Sichuan, 2018–2022):** 87% accuracy for abandoned cropland. — [MDPI Land 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-445X/13/5/586)
- **Sentinel-2 bidirectional LSTM:** up to 98.2% overall accuracy for detecting abandoned land. The authors tie it explicitly to monitoring CAP (EU farm subsidy) payments. — [Agronomy 2021](https://www2.mdpi.com/2073-4395/11/4/654)

### Inferences
- Sentinel-2 has 10 m pixels, so a 0.38 ha parcel (the Egri pre-consolidation average) is only about 38 pixels, with mixed pixels at its edges. That is workable for parcel-level classification but noisy for slivers.
- **Fusing optical data with Sentinel-1 radar** would help in cloudy seasons.
- Parcel boundaries can come from the cadastre or LPIS. Access to those layers is a dependency, possibly via MAFWE or AREC, the Agency for Real Estate Cadastre.
- The hard part is not "is this field unused?" but **"who owns it, are they alive, where are they, and will all co-owners sign?"** Satellite data does not solve that. The technical "moat" is therefore weak, since anyone can run open Sentinel-2 pipelines. The defensible asset would be the owner and heir database and the legal workflow.
- Calling a field abandoned when it is actually in a fallow rotation, or used as extensive pasture, is a real false-positive risk. That matters if Niva's maps are used for **tax enforcement**: owners could challenge assessments, so ground-truthing and legal standards of proof would be required.

### Gaps
- No Sentinel-2 abandonment study specific to North Macedonia or the Western Balkans was found.
- No validated accuracy figures were found for parcels under 0.5 ha in Mediterranean or Balkan landscapes.

## 6. Overall: the biggest risks, for benchmarking against new ideas

### Takeaway
Niva tackles a real, well-documented problem (about 32% abandonment, a large diaspora, extreme fragmentation) with feasible technology, and no direct local competitor was found. The main weaknesses:
- **Legal.** Leasing co-owned land needs unanimity under Article 37 of the Law on Ownership, and many heir parcels have unresolved ownership.
- **Economic.** Rents are low (about €60–200/ha/year) and parcels are tiny, so fee revenue per parcel is very small.
- **Public-funding dependence.** Every European analogue found is publicly or co-op funded.

### Cited Findings
- Leasing requires all co-owners; if they disagree, a court decides. — [Law on Ownership, Art. 37](https://business.gazibaba.gov.mk/media/pdf/zakonska_regulativa/pravni_raboti/sopstvenost_i_drugi_stvarni_prava/1-sopstvenost-i-drugi-stvarni-prava.pdf)
- State land tender floors are €60–120/ha. — [HortiDaily](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/6019876/released-call-for-the-lease-of-land-over-3-ha-in-macedonia/)
- Analogous land banks are publicly run or ministry-funded: Galicia ([Urban Agenda](https://www.urbanagenda.urban-initiative.eu/sites/default/files/2026-06/Banco%20de%20Terras%20de%20Galicia%2C%20Spain.pdf)), Austria ([EU CAP Network](https://eu-cap-network.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-12/eu-cap-network-tg-gr-perspektive-landwirtschaft-initiative.pdf)), Ireland ([AgriLand](https://www.agriland.ie/farming-news/macra-launches-land-mobility-service/)).
- The law has no legal definition of "abandoned land". — [FAO cc4778en [snippet]](https://www.fao.org/3/cc4778en/cc4778en.pdf)

### Inferences (risk ranking, highest first)
1. **Legal and title risk (high).** Unanimity is required to lease, inheritance is often unresolved, and owners are spread across many countries. Mitigations include:
   - a heir-tracing and remote power-of-attorney service, which could itself be the product;
   - partnering with notaries;
   - lobbying for a majority rule for agricultural leases, modelled on the majority rule in the Consolidation Law.
2. **Unit economics (high).** At about €100/ha rent and parcels under 1 ha, transaction costs swamp fees.
3. **Weak B2G hook (medium-high).**
   - With no legal definition of abandonment, municipal maps cannot easily be used as tax evidence.
   - Enforcement of the 3x tax is unverified.
   - Municipal budgets are small.
4. **Demand side (medium).**
   - The supply of young farmers is shrinking because of emigration.
   - Much abandoned land is in mountainous, low-quality zones.
   - State land is already offered cheaply through tenders.
5. **Technical moat (low risk, low defensibility).** Detection is feasible with open data, so it does not differentiate Niva.
6. **Upside factors:**
   - Strong fit with SDGs 2, 15 and 1, and with EU accession and IPARD agendas.
   - A clear partner ecosystem: FAO, EU, MAFWE.
   - No local competitor.
   - The diaspora (about 25% of the population) is a reachable, emotionally engaged customer base.
   - Regional replication is plausible, since Serbia, Bosnia, Albania and Bulgaria have similar issues. This is not verified here.

### Gaps
- No interview or survey data was found on diaspora owners' willingness to lease or pay a subscription.
- No data was found on how many young farmers in North Macedonia are actively seeking land.
- Legal conclusions rely on the 2001 base text of the Law on Ownership. They need confirmation by a North Macedonian lawyer against the current consolidated text and the Law on Agricultural Land.
